📘 English Explanation

Between the medieval and early modern periods, many different communities lived across the Indian subcontinent. Their economic activities, social organisation and political structures were not the same.

Some communities depended heavily on forests and pastoralism, while others practised agriculture, craft production and trade. Some groups moved from one place to another, whereas settled communities lived in villages and towns.

UPSC Idea: The study of tribes, nomadic groups and settled communities helps us understand the diversity of Indian society and the relationship between environment, economy, social organisation and political power.

1. Diversity of Communities

Medieval Indian society included farmers, artisans, traders, pastoralists, forest communities, hunters and many other groups.

These communities were connected through exchange. A forest community could supply forest products, pastoralists could provide animals and animal products, while settled agricultural communities could produce food grains.

🌲 Forest Communities

Often depended on forests for food, fuel, medicines, hunting and other resources.

🐄 Pastoral Communities

Moved with cattle, sheep, goats or other animals according to the availability of pasture and water.

🌾 Agricultural Communities

Practised cultivation and usually lived in relatively settled villages.

🛍️ Trading Communities

Connected villages, markets and towns through the movement of goods.

🌲 2. Tribal Societies

Tribal societies were often organised around kinship. Members of a tribe could be connected through family relationships, clans and common traditions.

Many tribal groups lived in forests, hills, deserts and other regions where agriculture or large settled states were less dominant.

Important Features

  • Kinship and clan relationships were important.
  • Many communities depended on forests and pastoralism.
  • Some practised shifting cultivation.
  • Some groups became settled agricultural communities.
  • Some tribal chiefs established political kingdoms.
  • Tribal groups were connected to neighbouring settled societies through trade.
Remember: “Tribal” does not mean that all tribal communities had exactly the same economic or political system. Their organisation varied greatly from region to region.

Kinship and Clans

A clan can be understood as a group of families claiming a common ancestry or connection. Several clans could form a larger tribal community.

🐪 3. Nomadic Communities

Nomadic communities moved from one region to another rather than living permanently in one place. Movement was often connected to pastoralism, seasonal resources and trade.

Pastoral Nomads

Pastoral nomads depended substantially on animals. Their movement allowed them to find fresh pasture and water.

Animals could provide milk, wool, meat, hides and transportation.

Nomadic Traders

Some mobile groups participated in trade. They carried goods between different settlements and markets.

Economic connection:
Nomadic groups were not necessarily isolated from settled society. Trade created strong links between mobile and settled communities.

🔄 4. Shifting Cultivation

Shifting cultivation involves cultivating a piece of land for a period and then moving to another area when soil fertility declines or other conditions make movement useful.

The practice was especially associated with forested and hilly regions. Different communities used different local methods.

🛡️ 5. The Gonds

The Gonds were an important tribal community associated with central India. Over time, some Gond groups developed larger political structures and kingdoms.

Gond Political Organisation

The growth of agriculture and political power enabled some chiefs to control larger territories.

Gond rulers developed systems of administration and maintained relations with neighbouring states and communities.

Gond Kingdoms

Gond kingdoms are an important example of how tribal political organisations could develop into more complex states.

👑 Political Power

Some tribal chiefs became powerful rulers and controlled territories.

🌾 Agriculture

Expansion of cultivation supported larger and more settled populations.

🏰 Forts

Political centres and fortified settlements could become symbols of authority.

🤝 Interaction

Gond rulers interacted with neighbouring kingdoms and wider political networks.

🐘 6. The Ahoms

The Ahoms established a powerful state in the Brahmaputra valley. Their history demonstrates how a community could transform its political and economic organisation over time.

Ahom State

The Ahom kingdom developed administrative institutions and military organisation. Agriculture was important for supporting the state.

Paik System

The Ahom state organised labour and military service through systems involving the paik population.

People could be required to provide labour or military service to the state. This helped the rulers mobilise resources.

UPSC Connection: The Ahom example illustrates the relationship between state formation, agriculture, labour organisation and military power.

🌾 7. Settled Agricultural Communities

Settled agricultural communities generally lived in villages where farming formed the economic foundation.

Agriculture produced food and sometimes a surplus that could support artisans, traders, rulers and other groups.

Village Economy

Villages included farmers as well as artisans and service providers. Their activities were connected through exchange and local economic relationships.

⚔️ 8. Tribes and States

The relationship between tribal communities and states was complex. Sometimes tribal groups resisted state expansion. In other situations, tribal chiefs entered political alliances or established their own states.

Community / Structure Possible Economic Base Political Relationship
Forest communities Forest resources, hunting, cultivation Independent or connected with states
Pastoral groups Livestock Trade, migration and political interaction
Settled farmers Agriculture Village and state structures
Tribal kingdoms Agriculture, resources and trade Organised political authority

🏷️ 9. Caste and Tribal Society

Tribal communities interacted with caste-based settled societies in many regions. These interactions could involve trade, migration, political alliances and cultural exchange.

Over long periods, social boundaries could change. Some tribal groups became part of wider political and social structures, while others retained distinct identities and institutions.

Key point: Indian society was not static. Social identities and political structures changed over time through migration, economic change, state formation and interaction between communities.

🧭 10. Regional Diversity

Different geographical environments produced different patterns of life. Forests, mountains, river valleys, deserts and fertile plains supported different economic strategies.

Region / Environment Possible Economic Activities
Forests Forest products, hunting, shifting cultivation
Grasslands Pastoralism and animal rearing
River valleys Agriculture, irrigation and trade
Hilly regions Mixed farming, pastoralism and forest activities
Trade routes Transport, exchange and commercial activities

⏳ Historical Development Flow

Stage 1: Communities developed different economic practices according to their environment.
Stage 2: Agriculture, pastoralism, hunting, forest activities and trade created interdependence.
Stage 3: Some tribal chiefs gained greater political authority.
Stage 4: Some groups developed larger political structures and kingdoms.
Stage 5: Interaction between tribes, settled communities and states transformed society over time.

🇮🇳 हिंदी में विस्तृत अध्ययन

1. जनजातीय समाज

मध्यकालीन भारत में अनेक प्रकार के समुदाय रहते थे। कुछ समुदाय जंगलों, पहाड़ी क्षेत्रों और अन्य प्राकृतिक क्षेत्रों पर निर्भर थे, जबकि कुछ समुदाय स्थायी कृषि करते थे।

जनजातीय समाज में रिश्तेदारी, परिवार और कबीलाई संबंधों का महत्वपूर्ण स्थान था। अलग-अलग जनजातियों की आर्थिक और राजनीतिक व्यवस्था एक जैसी नहीं थी।

2. खानाबदोश समुदाय

खानाबदोश समुदाय एक स्थान पर स्थायी रूप से नहीं रहते थे। कई पशुपालक समुदाय अपने पशुओं के लिए चारे और पानी की उपलब्धता के अनुसार स्थान बदलते थे।

कुछ घुमंतू समूह व्यापार से भी जुड़े हुए थे। वे अलग-अलग क्षेत्रों के बीच वस्तुओं को ले जाने में महत्वपूर्ण भूमिका निभा सकते थे।

3. स्थानांतरित कृषि

स्थानांतरित कृषि में किसी क्षेत्र में कुछ समय के लिए खेती की जाती है और परिस्थितियों के अनुसार दूसरे क्षेत्र की ओर चला जाता है।

यह व्यवस्था विशेष रूप से जंगलों और पहाड़ी क्षेत्रों के कुछ समुदायों से जुड़ी रही है।

4. गोंड

गोंड मध्य भारत का एक महत्वपूर्ण जनजातीय समुदाय था। समय के साथ कुछ गोंड समूहों ने बड़े राजनीतिक संगठन और राज्य विकसित किए।

कृषि के विस्तार, राजनीतिक शक्ति और पड़ोसी राज्यों के साथ संबंधों ने इनके राजनीतिक विकास को प्रभावित किया।

5. अहोम

अहोमों ने ब्रह्मपुत्र घाटी में एक शक्तिशाली राज्य का निर्माण किया। उनकी प्रशासनिक और सैन्य व्यवस्था राज्य निर्माण के अध्ययन के लिए महत्वपूर्ण है।

अहोम राज्य में कृषि और श्रम संगठन का महत्वपूर्ण स्थान था। paik व्यवस्था के माध्यम से लोगों से श्रम या सैन्य सेवा ली जा सकती थी।

6. स्थायी कृषि समाज

स्थायी कृषि करने वाले समुदाय गांवों में रहते थे। कृषि से प्राप्त उत्पादन गांव की अर्थव्यवस्था का आधार था।

अतिरिक्त उत्पादन से कारीगरों, व्यापारियों और अन्य समूहों को भी सहायता मिल सकती थी।

7. जनजाति और राज्य

जनजातीय समुदाय और राज्यों के संबंध हमेशा एक जैसे नहीं थे। कहीं संघर्ष हुआ, कहीं राजनीतिक सहयोग हुआ और कहीं जनजातीय प्रमुखों ने स्वयं राज्य स्थापित किए।

याद रखें: भारतीय समाज समय के साथ बदलता रहा। आर्थिक गतिविधियों, प्रवास, व्यापार, राजनीतिक शक्ति और अलग-अलग समुदायों के संपर्क से सामाजिक परिवर्तन हुए।

🎯 UPSC Important Points

📌 Tribal Society

Kinship, clans and local institutions were important features of many tribal communities.

📌 Nomads

Mobility was often connected with pastoralism, seasonal resources and trade.

📌 Gonds

Some Gond groups developed larger political structures and kingdoms.

📌 Ahoms

The Ahom state developed in the Brahmaputra valley with organised labour and military systems.

📌 Shifting Cultivation

A cultivation practice associated with movement between cultivated areas in certain ecological regions.

📌 State Formation

Some tribal chiefs became powerful rulers and established organised political structures.
