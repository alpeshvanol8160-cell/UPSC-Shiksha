The Rise of the Marathas

Class 8 History • Chapter 2 on UPSC Shiksha

An original, exam-oriented explanation of the rise of Maratha power, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj, Swarajya, administration, military strategy, maritime power, cultural revival and the Maratha legacy.

🧭 Chapter Overview

Who were the Marathas?

A major political community of the Deccan, especially associated with present-day Maharashtra and the Marathi language.

Central figure

Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj played a foundational role in organising Maratha power around the ideal of Swarajya.

Military strength

Forts, mobile warfare, knowledge of terrain, cavalry and naval power became important elements of Maratha strategy.

Political transformation

After Shivaji, the political system gradually became more decentralised, with the Peshwas and regional chiefs gaining major influence.

UPSC Connection: This chapter is useful for understanding state formation, regional powers, military geography, administration, maritime security, cultural revival and the transition from Mughal dominance to the rise of new political powers.

The rise of the Marathas cannot be explained by one battle or one ruler. It resulted from a combination of geography, social and cultural developments, political leadership, military organisation, administrative practices and later regional expansion.

🇬🇧 English Explanation

1. Who were the Marathas?

The Marathas emerged as a powerful political force in the Deccan. The region associated with them included large parts of present-day Maharashtra. Marathi became an important language of their cultural and political world.

Before the emergence of Shivaji, Maharashtra had experienced several political changes. The Yadavas had ruled from Devagiri, later known as Daulatabad, before their power was displaced by forces connected with the Delhi Sultanate.

Political changes did not erase cultural traditions. Religious and devotional traditions continued to develop and helped create a wider cultural environment in which ideas could circulate among ordinary people.

2. Bhakti and the Cultural Foundation

Maharashtra had a strong devotional tradition. Saints and poets such as Dnyaneshwar, Namdev, Tukaram and Ramdas contributed to Marathi literary and devotional culture.

The use of languages understood by ordinary people helped religious, ethical and social ideas travel beyond elite circles.

Important Insight: Political power often develops within a wider social and cultural environment. In the Maratha case, cultural confidence and regional identity contributed to political organisation.

3. Shivaji Maharaj and the Foundation of Swarajya

Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj was born in 1630 in the Bhonsle family. His parents were Shahji and Jijabai. He grew up in the Pune region, where political instability among the Deccan powers affected everyday life.

Shivaji began military activity at a young age. He strengthened his position by capturing and improving forts and gradually developed the political vision of Swarajya.

Swarajya can be understood broadly as the idea of self-rule. For Shivaji's state-building project, it involved political authority, economic organisation, protection of territory and a sense of cultural confidence.

4. Forts and Geography

Forts were extremely important to Maratha power. Mountain and coastal forts provided defensive positions, controlled routes and helped armies survive attacks by much larger forces.

Forts were not simply buildings. They were part of a wider military network connected with roads, passes, supplies, troops and communication.

5. Guerrilla Warfare

Shivaji used highly mobile forms of warfare suited to the geography of the Deccan. Small groups could use speed, surprise and knowledge of terrain against larger armies.

This type of warfare is often described as guerrilla warfare. It does not depend only on direct confrontation. Instead, mobility, intelligence, timing and terrain become major advantages.

Military Geography: A smaller force can sometimes compensate for numerical disadvantage by choosing favourable terrain, controlling movement and avoiding unnecessary direct battles.

6. Conflict with the Deccan Powers

Shivaji's expansion brought him into conflict with the Deccan Sultanates, including Bijapur. The confrontation with Afzal Khan near Pratapgad became one of the famous episodes of his early career.

The Maratha forces used the surrounding terrain and coordinated attacks to defeat the opposing force.

7. Conflict with the Mughals

Maratha expansion eventually brought Shivaji into direct conflict with the Mughal Empire.

Shaista Khan led a major Mughal campaign against Shivaji's territories. Shivaji responded with a surprise night attack on the Mughal camp.

The episode demonstrates an important principle of asymmetric warfare: a smaller force may attempt to compensate for inferior numbers through speed, intelligence and surprise.

8. Surat and the Mughal Response

Shivaji attacked Surat, an important Mughal port and commercial centre. The event had both economic and political significance because Surat was wealthy and its attack challenged Mughal prestige.

The Mughal response included a campaign led by Jai Singh. Shivaji eventually entered into the Treaty of Purandar and later travelled to the Mughal court at Agra.

9. Escape from Agra

At Agra, Shivaji became increasingly dissatisfied with the treatment he received and was placed under house arrest.

He and his son Sambhaji eventually escaped using a carefully planned deception involving baskets of gifts.

Strategic Lesson: Leadership is not limited to battlefield strength. Planning, intelligence, deception and adaptability can also determine outcomes.

10. Coronation at Raigad

In 1674, Shivaji was formally crowned at Raigad. The coronation symbolised sovereign political authority and gave institutional legitimacy to the Maratha kingdom.

Shivaji later expanded towards southern India. This southern expansion provided additional strategic depth and resources for the Maratha state.

11. The Marathas after Shivaji

Shivaji died in 1680. His successors faced continued Mughal pressure. Sambhaji became ruler, followed by Rajaram.

Aurangzeb personally directed prolonged campaigns in the Deccan. Despite the enormous Mughal military effort, the Marathas continued to resist.

Tarabai played an important leadership role after Rajaram's death. Maratha resistance eventually transformed into a broader expansion into Mughal territories.

12. Rise of the Peshwas

Over time, the political structure changed. The Peshwa, originally an important ministerial office, became increasingly powerful.

Peshwa Bajirao I and his successor Nanasaheb played major roles in Maratha expansion across large parts of India.

This expansion also produced a more decentralised political system. Regional chiefs gained increasing autonomy.

13. Pan-Indian Expansion

By the eighteenth century, Maratha influence extended across large parts of central and northern India.

Maratha power reached areas as far north-west as Lahore, Attock and Peshawar for periods of time.

The Marathas suffered a major defeat at the Third Battle of Panipat in 1761 but recovered under later leadership.

Mahadji Shinde played an important role in rebuilding Maratha influence and the Marathas regained control of Delhi in 1771.

14. Anglo-Maratha Wars

By the late eighteenth century, the Marathas had become one of the most important rivals of the British in India.

Three Anglo-Maratha Wars were fought between 1775 and 1818. Internal divisions among Maratha powers and British organisational and technological advantages contributed to the eventual end of Maratha political dominance.

UPSC Analysis: The decline of the Marathas should not be explained simply as “military weakness”. Political fragmentation, rivalry among chiefs, changing warfare and British organisational advantages were important factors.

15. Civilian Administration

Shivaji developed a relatively centralised administrative structure. He attempted to reduce the excessive concentration of hereditary official power and used salaries from the state treasury for officials.

Officials could also be transferred, reducing the possibility that they would become independently powerful.

The Ashtapradhan Mandal, or council of eight ministers, assisted the ruler in administration.

16. Revenue System

Maratha authorities collected revenues from territories under their control and also levied taxes from some territories that were not directly incorporated.

Two important terms are:

  • Chauth: traditionally associated with a quarter or 25% levy.
  • Sardeshmukhi: an additional levy traditionally associated with 10%.

These systems were connected with protection arrangements and the expanding political influence of the Marathas.

17. Military Organisation

The Maratha armed forces included infantry, cavalry and naval forces.

Military element Role
Infantry Foot soldiers used in forts, campaigns and battlefield operations.
Cavalry Mobility, rapid movement, raids and wider campaigns.
Navy Protection of the western coast and maritime interests.
Forts Defence, strategic control, storage and military shelter.

18. Bārgīrs and Shiledārs

Maratha cavalry included different categories of soldiers. Bārgīrs received horses and equipment from the state, whereas Shiledārs generally supplied their own horses and equipment.

19. Maritime Power

Shivaji recognised the importance of the western coast and established a navy.

Coastal forts and naval forces helped protect maritime interests. Later, Kanhoji Angre became a prominent Maratha naval leader.

The Marathas challenged European naval dominance and developed their own maritime networks despite not possessing technology identical to European fleets.

20. Judicial Administration

Local institutions and officials played roles in maintaining justice and order. Panchayats and other local authorities could participate in dispute resolution.

Urban centres also had officials such as the kotwal responsible for aspects of law and order.

21. Trade Networks

Shivaji encouraged trade and maritime commerce. Maratha-linked ships travelled to ports across the Indian Ocean, connecting western India with wider commercial networks.

Roads, bridges and ferry networks also supported movement of people, goods and armies.

22. Women and Maratha Power

Women played significant roles in Maratha political history.

Tarabai

She provided strong leadership during a critical phase of Maratha resistance and helped organise campaigns against Mughal territories.

Ahilyabai Holkar

She governed effectively and became associated with public works, temples, roads, wells, ghats and support for crafts.

23. Cultural Revival

Maratha rule also supported cultural and literary development. The Modi script became important for administrative correspondence.

In Thanjavur, Maratha rulers interacted with existing Tamil and Telugu traditions, contributing to a multilingual cultural environment.

Serfoji II became particularly known for supporting literature, music, medicine, education and printing.

24. The Maratha Legacy

The Marathas challenged Mughal dominance and became one of the most important political forces before British supremacy.

Their legacy includes state-building, fort architecture, military organisation, maritime activity, administrative innovations and cultural patronage.

Core Idea: The history of the Marathas is not only the story of Shivaji or battles. It is also a story of state formation, regional identity, administration, economic networks, military geography and cultural confidence.

🇮🇳 हिंदी में विस्तृत समझ

1. मराठे कौन थे?

मराठे दक्कन क्षेत्र, विशेष रूप से वर्तमान महाराष्ट्र से जुड़े एक महत्वपूर्ण राजनीतिक और सामाजिक समूह के रूप में उभरे। मराठी भाषा ने उनकी सांस्कृतिक पहचान में महत्वपूर्ण भूमिका निभाई।

महाराष्ट्र में समय के साथ कई राजनीतिक शक्तियों का प्रभाव रहा। यादवों की राजधानी देवगिरि थी, जिसे बाद में दौलताबाद कहा गया। बाद में दिल्ली सल्तनत से जुड़ी शक्तियों ने इस क्षेत्र पर प्रभाव स्थापित किया।

2. भक्ति परंपरा का प्रभाव

महाराष्ट्र में भक्ति परंपरा का मजबूत विकास हुआ। ज्ञानेश्वर, नामदेव, तुकाराम और रामदास जैसे संतों ने स्थानीय भाषा में धार्मिक और सामाजिक विचारों को लोगों तक पहुँचाया।

इससे एक मजबूत सांस्कृतिक वातावरण बना। आगे चलकर इसी वातावरण ने मराठों के राजनीतिक संगठन को भी समर्थन दिया।

3. छत्रपति शिवाजी महाराज का उदय

शिवाजी महाराज का जन्म 1630 में भोंसले परिवार में हुआ। उनके पिता शाहजी और माता जीजाबाई थीं।

पुणे क्षेत्र में उनके प्रारंभिक जीवन ने उन्हें दक्कन की राजनीतिक परिस्थितियों और स्थानीय भूगोल को समझने का अवसर दिया।

उन्होंने कम उम्र में किलों पर नियंत्रण स्थापित करना शुरू किया और धीरे-धीरे एक स्वतंत्र राज्य की कल्पना विकसित की।

4. स्वराज्य की अवधारणा

स्वराज्य का सामान्य अर्थ है — अपने शासन की स्थापना। शिवाजी के संदर्भ में यह केवल राजनीतिक सत्ता नहीं थी बल्कि राज्य की सुरक्षा, प्रशासन, आर्थिक व्यवस्था और सांस्कृतिक आत्मविश्वास से भी जुड़ी थी।

5. किलों का महत्व

मराठा शक्ति में किलों की भूमिका बहुत महत्वपूर्ण थी। पहाड़ी और समुद्री किले सेना को सुरक्षा देते थे और महत्वपूर्ण मार्गों पर नियंत्रण रखने में मदद करते थे।

याद रखें: मराठा शक्ति को समझने के लिए केवल युद्धों को नहीं, बल्कि भूगोल + किले + गतिशील सेना + प्रशासन को साथ में समझें।

6. गुरिल्ला युद्ध

शिवाजी की सैन्य रणनीति में तेज गति, अचानक हमला, छोटे सैन्य दल और स्थानीय भूगोल का उपयोग महत्वपूर्ण था।

बड़ी सेना के साथ सीधे लंबे युद्ध के बजाय अनुकूल स्थान और समय चुनना छोटी सेना के लिए रणनीतिक लाभ पैदा कर सकता था।

7. मुगलों से संघर्ष

मराठा विस्तार के कारण मुगल साम्राज्य के साथ संघर्ष बढ़ा। शाइस्ता खान के विरुद्ध शिवाजी का रात्रि हमला उनकी गतिशील रणनीति का प्रसिद्ध उदाहरण माना जाता है।

सूरत पर हमला आर्थिक और राजनीतिक दोनों दृष्टियों से महत्वपूर्ण था। इससे मुगल प्रतिष्ठा को चुनौती मिली और आगे मुगल प्रतिक्रिया तेज हुई।

8. आगरा से बच निकलना

आगरा में शिवाजी को नजरबंद किया गया। उन्होंने योजनाबद्ध तरीके से अपने पुत्र संभाजी के साथ वहाँ से निकलने में सफलता प्राप्त की।

यह घटना बताती है कि राजनीतिक संघर्ष में केवल युद्ध शक्ति ही नहीं, बल्कि योजना, बुद्धिमत्ता और रणनीतिक सोच भी महत्वपूर्ण होती है।

9. 1674 का राज्याभिषेक

1674 में रायगढ़ में शिवाजी का राज्याभिषेक हुआ। इससे उनकी संप्रभु राजनीतिक सत्ता को औपचारिक वैधता मिली।

इसके बाद दक्षिण भारत की ओर विस्तार ने मराठों को अतिरिक्त रणनीतिक गहराई प्रदान की।

10. शिवाजी के बाद

शिवाजी की मृत्यु के बाद संभाजी और फिर राजाराम ने नेतृत्व किया। मुगल सम्राट औरंगजेब ने दक्कन में लंबे समय तक अभियान चलाया, लेकिन मराठा प्रतिरोध समाप्त नहीं कर सका।

राजाराम के बाद ताराबाई ने महत्वपूर्ण नेतृत्व दिया। मराठा शक्ति धीरे-धीरे रक्षात्मक संघर्ष से आगे बढ़कर मुगल क्षेत्रों में विस्तार करने लगी।

11. पेशवाओं का उदय

समय के साथ पेशवा पद अत्यंत शक्तिशाली हो गया। बाजीराव प्रथम और नानासाहेब पेशवा ने मराठा विस्तार में महत्वपूर्ण भूमिका निभाई।

जैसे-जैसे मराठा शक्ति का विस्तार हुआ, राजनीतिक व्यवस्था अधिक विकेंद्रीकृत होती गई और विभिन्न क्षेत्रीय सरदारों की शक्ति बढ़ी।

12. पानीपत और पुनरुत्थान

1761 का तीसरा पानीपत युद्ध मराठों के लिए एक बड़ी हार थी। लेकिन यह उनकी कहानी का अंत नहीं था।

बाद में माधवराव प्रथम और महादजी शिंदे जैसे नेताओं के समय मराठा प्रभाव फिर मजबूत हुआ।

13. अंग्रेजों से संघर्ष

अठारहवीं शताब्दी के उत्तरार्ध में मराठे अंग्रेजों के सबसे महत्वपूर्ण भारतीय प्रतिद्वंद्वियों में से एक थे।

1775 से 1818 के बीच तीन एंग्लो-मराठा युद्ध हुए। आंतरिक राजनीतिक विभाजन और ब्रिटिश संगठनात्मक तथा तकनीकी बढ़त ने अंततः मराठा राजनीतिक प्रभुत्व को समाप्त कर दिया।

14. मराठा प्रशासन

शिवाजी ने अपेक्षाकृत केंद्रीकृत प्रशासन विकसित किया। अधिकारियों की नियुक्ति, वेतन, स्थानांतरण और जिम्मेदारियों पर विशेष ध्यान दिया गया।

अष्टप्रधान मंडल में आठ प्रमुख मंत्री प्रशासन में सहायता करते थे।

15. चौथ और सरदेशमुखी

  • चौथ: सामान्यतः 25 प्रतिशत की कर-व्यवस्था से जुड़ा शब्द।
  • सरदेशमुखी: सामान्यतः अतिरिक्त 10 प्रतिशत लेवी से जुड़ा शब्द।

16. मराठा सेना

मराठा सैन्य व्यवस्था में पैदल सेना, घुड़सवार सेना और नौसेना शामिल थी। किले और तेज गति वाली सेना उनकी रणनीतिक शक्ति के महत्वपूर्ण हिस्से थे।

17. नौसेना

पश्चिमी तट की सुरक्षा के लिए शिवाजी ने नौसेना विकसित की। बाद में कान्होजी आंग्रे प्रमुख मराठा नौसैनिक नेताओं में उभरे।

मराठों ने यूरोपीय समुद्री शक्तियों को चुनौती दी और पश्चिमी तट पर अपनी स्थिति मजबूत की।

18. ताराबाई और अहिल्याबाई होलकर

ताराबाई ने मुगल दबाव के समय मराठा प्रतिरोध का नेतृत्व किया।

अहिल्याबाई होलकर प्रभावी शासन, जनकल्याण, मंदिरों, घाटों, कुओं और सड़कों जैसे निर्माण कार्यों के लिए प्रसिद्ध हुईं।

19. सांस्कृतिक योगदान

मराठा काल में मराठी भाषा, साहित्य और प्रशासनिक परंपराओं को प्रोत्साहन मिला। मोदी लिपि प्रशासनिक पत्राचार में महत्वपूर्ण थी।

तंजावुर में मराठा शासकों ने तमिल, तेलुगु और मराठी परंपराओं के साथ मिलकर एक बहुभाषी सांस्कृतिक वातावरण को बढ़ावा दिया।

20. मराठा विरासत

मराठों ने मुगल प्रभुत्व को चुनौती दी और ब्रिटिश प्रभुत्व से पहले भारत की सबसे महत्वपूर्ण राजनीतिक शक्तियों में से एक बने।

उनकी विरासत में राज्य निर्माण, प्रशासन, किले, सैन्य रणनीति, नौसैनिक शक्ति, व्यापार और सांस्कृतिक संरक्षण शामिल हैं।

⚡ Quick Revision

  • मराठा शक्ति का प्रमुख क्षेत्र — दक्कन/महाराष्ट्र।
  • शिवाजी का जन्म — 1630।
  • मुख्य राजनीतिक विचार — स्वराज्य।
  • राज्याभिषेक — 1674, रायगढ़।
  • मुख्य सैन्य आधार — किले और गतिशील सेना।
  • महत्वपूर्ण रणनीति — गुरिल्ला शैली का युद्ध।
  • प्रमुख नौसैनिक नेता — कान्होजी आंग्रे।
  • अष्टप्रधान — प्रशासनिक मंत्रिपरिषद।
  • चौथ — 25% से जुड़ी लेवी।
  • सरदेशमुखी — अतिरिक्त 10% से जुड़ी लेवी।
  • तीसरा पानीपत — 1761।
  • दिल्ली पर पुनः मराठा प्रभाव — 1771।
