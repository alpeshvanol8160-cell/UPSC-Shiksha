    🏺 Ancient India

    Major civilizations, Vedic traditions, religions, kingdoms, society and economy.

    🏺 🐂 📜 🛕
    Ancient India — Civilization • Society • Religion • Economy
    Aspect Important Points
    Indus Valley Civilization Urban settlements, planned cities, drainage systems, craft production and long-distance trade.
    Vedic Age Early Vedic and Later Vedic phases, changing political, social and economic institutions.
    Buddhism Teachings associated with Gautama Buddha, Four Noble Truths and Eightfold Path.
    Jainism Mahavira, non-violence, truth and the path of liberation.
    Mauryan Empire Chandragupta Maurya, Ashoka, administration and Ashokan inscriptions.
    Gupta Period Developments in literature, science, mathematics, art and architecture.

    📜 Vedic Age — Quick Study Table

    Aspect Details
    Political Life Institutions such as Sabha and Samiti are discussed in the study of early Vedic polity.
    Economy Cattle played an important role; agriculture became increasingly important in later phases.
    Social Life Kinship, family and changing social institutions were important features.
    Religion Rituals and deities associated with natural forces were important in early Vedic traditions.

    🕊️ Freedom Struggle

    Major phases, movements, organisations and contributors.

    1857 — Revolt Major uprising against British rule with different regional centres and causes.
    1885 — Indian National Congress Formation of an important political organisation in the national movement.
    1905 — Swadeshi Movement Nationalist mobilisation associated with the partition of Bengal.
    1919 — Rowlatt Satyagraha & Jallianwala Bagh Important developments in the nationalist movement.
    1920–22 — Non-Cooperation Movement Mass mobilisation under Gandhi's leadership.
    1930 — Civil Disobedience Movement Associated with the Salt March and wider civil resistance.
    1942 — Quit India Movement Major mass movement demanding an end to British rule.
    1947 — Independence India became independent on 15 August 1947.
