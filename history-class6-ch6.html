📖 English Explanation

Around the sixth century BCE, many people in the Indian subcontinent began asking new questions about life, suffering, death, rituals, social differences and the purpose of human existence.

These questions encouraged the growth of several philosophical and religious traditions. Buddhism and Jainism became especially important, while other traditions such as the Upanishadic thinkers and different shramana traditions also explored alternative ideas.

⭐ Core Idea: The period was not simply about the rise of two religions. It was a wider period of intellectual and social questioning in which people explored different paths to knowledge, ethical living and liberation.

☸️ The Buddha

The Buddha is traditionally identified as Siddhartha Gautama. He was associated with the Shakya clan and is traditionally said to have been born at Lumbini.

According to Buddhist traditions, he lived a protected life during his early years. Encounters with old age, illness, death and an ascetic deeply affected him.

Important: The traditional Buddhist account uses these encounters to explain Siddhartha's growing concern with suffering and his search for a solution.

🛤️ Buddha's Life Journey

1. Lumbini

Traditional birthplace associated with Siddhartha Gautama.

2. Renunciation

He left his privileged life to search for answers to suffering.

3. Enlightenment — Bodh Gaya

He attained enlightenment and became known as the Buddha, meaning "Awakened One".

4. First Sermon — Sarnath

He began teaching the path he had discovered.

5. Mahaparinirvana — Kushinagara

Traditional Buddhist accounts associate his final passing with Kushinagara.

🕊️ Mahavira and Jainism

Mahavira is traditionally regarded in Jain tradition as the 24th Tirthankara. He belonged to a kshatriya family and is associated with the Vajji region.

He renounced worldly life and practiced severe austerities before attaining spiritual knowledge known in Jain tradition as kevala jnana.

Central Jain emphasis: Non-violence, self-discipline and reducing attachment were central to the Jain path.

👥 The Sangha

The Buddha's followers formed a community known as the Sangha. It included monks and, over time, nuns.

Members followed rules concerning conduct, discipline and community life. These rules are associated with the Buddhist Vinaya tradition.

👨‍🦲
Monks

Followed disciplined monastic life.

👩‍🦲

🍚

Monasteries

Became important centres of teaching, residence and learning.

🏛️ Monasteries

Buddhist and Jain teachings could attract people from different social and economic backgrounds. Merchants, artisans and some rulers supported monks and monasteries.

Historical participation was complex. It is better to say that these traditions created alternative religious and ethical paths and questioned some existing practices, rather than claiming that they completely removed social hierarchy.

🧠 Other Thinkers and Traditions

The sixth century BCE was a period of intellectual diversity. Buddhists and Jains were part of a broader group of thinkers and movements often discussed under the term shramana.

The Upanishads also contain discussions about the nature of reality, the self, knowledge, karma and liberation.

UPSC Tip: Do not treat "Upanishadic thought" as one completely uniform doctrine. The Upanishads contain diverse philosophical discussions.

🎯 UPSC Important Points

  • छठी शताब्दी ईसा पूर्व नए धार्मिक और दार्शनिक प्रश्नों का महत्वपूर्ण दौर था।
  • बुद्ध का मूल नाम सिद्धार्थ गौतम माना जाता है।
  • बोध गया बुद्ध के ज्ञान प्राप्ति से जुड़ा प्रमुख स्थान है।
  • सारनाथ बुद्ध के प्रथम उपदेश से जुड़ा है।
  • कुशीनगर उनके महापरिनिर्वाण से जुड़ा है।
  • चार आर्य सत्य बौद्ध दर्शन की महत्वपूर्ण आधारभूत शिक्षाएँ हैं।
  • Eightfold Path दुख से मुक्ति के अभ्यास का मार्ग बताता है।
  • महावीर जैन परंपरा में 24वें तीर्थंकर माने जाते हैं।
  • अहिंसा जैन धर्म का अत्यंत महत्वपूर्ण सिद्धांत है।
  • बौद्ध संघ में भिक्षु और भिक्षुणियों की संगठित परंपरा विकसित हुई।
  • मठ धार्मिक शिक्षा और सामुदायिक जीवन के महत्वपूर्ण केन्द्र बने।
  • व्यापारियों और कुछ शासकों ने बौद्ध एवं जैन समुदायों को संरक्षण दिया।
  • उपनिषदों में आत्मा, कर्म, ज्ञान और मुक्ति जैसे प्रश्नों पर चर्चा मिलती है।
