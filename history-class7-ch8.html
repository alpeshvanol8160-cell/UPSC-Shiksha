📖 English Explanation

1. Understanding the Eighteenth Century

The eighteenth century was a period of major political change in the Indian subcontinent. The Mughal Empire had reached a very large territorial extent, but its ability to control every region became weaker after the death of Aurangzeb in 1707.

Instead of seeing this period simply as a story of one empire disappearing, it is more useful to understand it as a period of political reorganisation. Several regional powers became stronger while the authority of the Mughal emperor became increasingly limited.

Core Idea: Political power did not suddenly disappear. It was redistributed among regional rulers, provincial governors, military groups, zamindars and other political communities.

⚠️ 2. Crisis of the Mughal Empire

Why did the Mughal Empire face difficulties?

Aurangzeb's long military campaigns, particularly in the Deccan, placed heavy pressure on imperial finances and military resources.

After his death, later emperors often struggled to maintain control over powerful nobles and distant provinces.

🏙️ 5. Hyderabad, Awadh and Bengal

Hyderabad

The Deccan became an important centre of political activity. Asaf Jah, who became the first Nizam of Hyderabad, established a strong political base in the region.

Awadh

Awadh developed into a powerful regional state under its governors. Its location and agricultural resources made it politically and economically important.

Bengal

Bengal possessed rich agricultural resources and important commercial connections. Its provincial rulers increasingly exercised independent authority.

👑 7. Rajputs and Regional Power

Rajput rulers had long maintained important political traditions in Rajasthan. During the eighteenth century, several Rajput states continued to exercise substantial regional authority.

Rajput rulers also participated in the changing political system through alliances, warfare and negotiations with larger powers.

Jaipur and Sawai Jai Singh

Sawai Jai Singh II was an important ruler of Jaipur. He was also interested in astronomy and established observatories known as Jantar Mantars.

Historical connection: Political authority and cultural activity often developed together. Courts could support architecture, science, literature and other forms of knowledge.

🛡️ 8. The Sikhs

The Sikh community developed an increasingly organised political identity in the Punjab during the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries.

The formation of the Khalsa under Guru Gobind Singh in 1699 was an important development in Sikh history.

After Guru Gobind Singh's death, Banda Bahadur led a rebellion against Mughal authority. Later, Sikh groups known as misls became important political and military organisations in Punjab.

⚔️ 9. The Marathas

The Marathas emerged as a major political power in western India. Their rise was connected with the political traditions established under Shivaji and the later expansion of Maratha power.

Peshwas

The Peshwas became increasingly important in Maratha administration. Under their leadership, Maratha influence expanded into several parts of the subcontinent.

Chauth and Sardeshmukhi

Maratha political expansion was associated with systems of revenue collection such as chauth and sardeshmukhi in territories under their influence.

Important: Maratha expansion was not simply a military story. Administration, revenue, alliances, local political relationships and military organisation all played roles.

🌾 11. The Jats

The Jats became an important political force in the region around Delhi and the western Gangetic-Yamuna plains.

Bharatpur

Under leaders such as Suraj Mal, the Jat state centred around Bharatpur became an important regional power.

The Bharatpur state illustrates how local social groups could transform themselves into organised political powers during a period of changing imperial authority.

Concept: Social organisation + control of resources + military organisation + political leadership could contribute to the formation of a regional state.

💰 13. Merchants, Bankers and Political Power

Eighteenth-century politics was also connected with economic networks. Merchants and bankers could provide financial support to rulers and political groups.

Control over revenue and trade therefore mattered for political stability. Regional courts also became centres of commercial and cultural activity.

UPSC Concept: Political history and economic history are closely connected. Revenue, banking, trade and military expenditure influenced state formation.

🌍 14. Foreign Invasions

The weakening of Mughal political authority coincided with invasions from outside the empire.

Nadir Shah

Nadir Shah invaded north India and attacked Delhi in 1739. The invasion exposed the vulnerability of the Mughal political system and caused enormous loss of wealth.

Ahmad Shah Abdali

Ahmad Shah Abdali also invaded north India multiple times. His interventions became part of the wider political competition in the eighteenth century.

Why it matters: Foreign invasions added pressure to an already fragmented political system, but they were only one part of the larger story of eighteenth-century change.

⚔️ 15. Third Battle of Panipat — 1761

The Third Battle of Panipat was fought in 1761 between the Marathas and the Afghan forces led by Ahmad Shah Abdali, along with his Indian allies.

The battle resulted in heavy casualties and significantly affected the balance of political power in northern India.

🇮🇳 हिंदी में समझें

1. अठारहवीं शताब्दी का राजनीतिक परिवर्तन

1707 में औरंगज़ेब की मृत्यु के बाद मुगल साम्राज्य की केंद्रीय शक्ति धीरे-धीरे कमजोर होने लगी। इसका अर्थ यह नहीं था कि मुगल सत्ता तुरंत समाप्त हो गई। बल्कि अलग-अलग क्षेत्रों में स्थानीय और प्रांतीय शक्तियाँ अधिक प्रभावशाली होने लगीं।

मुख्य विचार: अठारहवीं शताब्दी को केवल “मुगल साम्राज्य का पतन” कहना पर्याप्त नहीं है। यह राजनीतिक शक्ति के पुनर्गठन का भी काल था।

2. मुगल साम्राज्य का संकट

दक्कन में लंबे युद्धों ने सैन्य और आर्थिक संसाधनों पर दबाव डाला। बाद के मुगल शासकों को शक्तिशाली अमीरों और प्रांतीय अधिकारियों को नियंत्रित करने में कठिनाई हुई।

3. नए क्षेत्रीय राज्य

हैदराबाद, अवध और बंगाल जैसे क्षेत्रों में प्रांतीय शासकों ने अधिक स्वायत्तता प्राप्त की। राजस्थान के राजपूत राज्यों, पंजाब के सिख समूहों, मराठों और जाटों ने भी महत्वपूर्ण राजनीतिक शक्ति विकसित की।

4. सिख

गुरु गोबिंद सिंह द्वारा 1699 में खालसा की स्थापना सिख इतिहास में महत्वपूर्ण घटना थी। बाद में बंदा बहादुर के नेतृत्व में मुगल सत्ता के विरुद्ध संघर्ष हुआ। अठारहवीं शताब्दी में सिख मिसलों ने पंजाब में राजनीतिक और सैन्य शक्ति का विकास किया।

5. मराठा

मराठों की शक्ति पश्चिमी भारत से बढ़ी और बाद में उनका प्रभाव कई क्षेत्रों तक फैला। पेशवाओं ने प्रशासन और राजनीतिक विस्तार में महत्वपूर्ण भूमिका निभाई।

6. जाट

जाटों ने दिल्ली और आगरा के आसपास के क्षेत्रों में राजनीतिक शक्ति विकसित की। भरतपुर राज्य इसका महत्वपूर्ण उदाहरण बना। सूरज मल इसके प्रमुख नेताओं में से एक थे।

7. बाहरी आक्रमण

1739 में नादिर शाह ने दिल्ली पर आक्रमण किया। अहमद शाह अब्दाली ने भी उत्तर भारत में कई बार हस्तक्षेप किया। इन घटनाओं ने राजनीतिक अस्थिरता को बढ़ाया।

8. पानीपत का तीसरा युद्ध

1761 में मराठों और अहमद शाह अब्दाली के नेतृत्व वाली सेनाओं के बीच तीसरा पानीपत युद्ध हुआ। इस युद्ध ने उत्तर भारत की राजनीतिक शक्ति-संतुलन को प्रभावित किया।
